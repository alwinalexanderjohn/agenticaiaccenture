TOPIC: Seasonal Energy Management Strategies
SOURCE: EcoHome Knowledge Base — Seasonal Energy Management

Energy needs and optimization strategies change significantly with the seasons. Tailoring your energy
management to seasonal patterns can reduce annual energy costs by an additional 15–25%.

SUMMER ENERGY MANAGEMENT (June–August)
Challenges: Peak cooling demand, high TOU prices, AC cycling

Solar Optimization:
- Long sunny days (14–16 hours) provide maximum solar generation; 10 kW system averages 60–70 kWh/day
- Schedule all major loads for 10 AM–3 PM: pool pump, HVAC pre-cooling, EV charging, dishwasher
- Solar export value highest in summer in net-metered regions (high grid demand = high SREC prices)

Cooling Strategies:
- Pre-cool to 72°F by 4 PM using solar, then let temperature rise to 76–78°F during peak hours
- Use ceiling fans (saves ~$0.10/hour vs running AC)
- Grill outside instead of oven — oven adds 3,000 BTU of heat that AC must remove
- Close east-facing blinds in morning, west-facing in afternoon; reduces cooling load 10–15%
- Install attic fans: remove attic heat (150°F+) reduces AC load by 10–15%

EV Charging in Summer:
- EV battery performance is best at 60–80°F; summer heat slightly reduces range
- Charge in shade or garage when possible to reduce thermal management energy
- Use vehicle solar charging schedule to align with 10 AM–2 PM solar peak

FALL ENERGY MANAGEMENT (September–November)
Opportunities: Moderate temperatures, lower HVAC need, good solar days

- Shoulder season: HVAC off for 1–3 months in mild climates; lowest energy bills
- Use this period to: weatherize (seal gaps, insulate), schedule HVAC tune-up, clean solar panels
- Gradually shift water heater schedule later into day as sunset moves earlier
- EV charging window expands as evening temperatures drop (battery charges more efficiently in cool weather)
- Harvest mode: Ensure battery charges to 100% daily while solar is still strong (before rainy season)

WINTER ENERGY MANAGEMENT (December–February)
Challenges: Short days, low solar angle, heating demand, reduced solar output

Solar Production:
- 10 kW system generates 25–40 kWh/day in winter vs 60–70 kWh/day in summer (40–50% reduction)
- Low sun angle means panel tilt matters more: optimal winter tilt = latitude + 15°
- Snow accumulation: 1 inch of snow can reduce output 40%; 2+ inches = near zero; clear manually if safe
- Morning shading from trees (bare in winter) may be worse than summer; check panel performance logs

Heating Optimization:
- Heat pump efficiency (COP) drops in extreme cold: at 5°F, COP may fall to 1.5–2.0 vs 3.5 at 45°F
- Backup resistance heat kicks in below 30–35°F threshold; very expensive ($0.15–0.32/kWh equivalent)
- Consider weather-adaptive thermostat schedules that account for outdoor temperature
- Water pipes in exterior walls: let faucet drip below 20°F to prevent freeze (cheaper than pipe repair)
- Electric blankets (60W) are far more efficient than raising room temperature for sleep comfort

EV in Winter:
- Cold batteries have reduced range (15–30% less at 20°F)
- Pre-condition battery and cabin while plugged in (not on battery) saves 5–10 miles of range
- Charge to 80–90% in winter to maintain battery warmth; full charge recommended before long trips
- Allow extra charging time: cold slows charge acceptance rate

SPRING ENERGY MANAGEMENT (March–May)
Opportunities: Rising solar production, mild temperatures, pre-summer preparation

- Clean panels after winter pollen season (April–May in most regions)
- Schedule HVAC maintenance before summer cooling season
- Re-enable irrigation controllers; set smart irrigation based on weather data
- Window washing increases natural light → less artificial lighting needed
- Gradually shift EV charging back to solar hours as daylight extends
- Evaluate past year's energy bills; set energy goals for coming year

SEASONAL RATE CHANGES
Many utilities have seasonal rate tiers:
- Summer rates: Often higher; utilities pass on higher wholesale energy costs
- Winter rates: Often lower in sun-belt states where natural gas is primary source
- Consult your utility's rate schedule (available online) to understand seasonal bill drivers
- Consider TOU plans that provide cheapest overnight rates for EV charging year-round

ENERGY BUDGET PLANNING
Annual energy budget framework:
- Month 1–2 (Jan–Feb): Highest gas/heat costs; lower AC; baseline electric $80–120
- Month 6–8 (Jun–Aug): Highest AC costs; highest solar offset; net bill near $0 with good solar
- Month 10–12 (Oct–Dec): Rising heating; falling solar; budget for higher bills
- Annual solar offset goal: 80–100% of annual grid consumption
- Track monthly: production, consumption, grid import, grid export → optimize scheduling monthly
