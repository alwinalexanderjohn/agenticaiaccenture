TOPIC: HVAC Optimization Strategies for Smart Homes
SOURCE: EcoHome Knowledge Base — HVAC Optimization

HVAC systems (heating, ventilation, and air conditioning) are the single largest energy consumer in most
homes, accounting for 40–50% of total electricity use. Optimizing HVAC can yield the largest savings of
any home improvement measure.

PRE-CONDITIONING STRATEGY
Pre-cool or pre-heat your home before peak electricity pricing begins (typically 5 PM). Start cooling
at 3–4 PM when solar generation is still high and rates are lower. This "thermal mass" strategy stores
coolness in walls, furniture, and floors. After 5 PM, raise the thermostat 2–3°F — the home stays
comfortable for 1–2 hours without active cooling.
Savings: $80–150/year in hot climates.

ZONING AND OCCUPANCY-BASED CONTROL
Modern smart thermostats (Ecobee, Nest) with room sensors adjust temperature based on where people are,
not just the hallway where the thermostat is located. Unused rooms can be closed off (in duct systems)
or set to wider temperature bands. Multi-zone systems with individual room control can save 20–30%.

HEAT PUMP TECHNOLOGY
Heat pumps are 3–4x more efficient than resistance heaters. A heat pump delivers 3 kWh of heat for
every 1 kWh of electricity consumed (COP of 3). At $0.15/kWh average rate:
- Resistance heater: 1 kWh → 1 kWh heat → $0.15/kWh of heat
- Heat pump: 1 kWh → 3 kWh heat → $0.05/kWh of heat
Annual savings switching from electric resistance to heat pump: $600–1,200 for average home.

HUMIDITY MANAGEMENT
Maintaining 40–50% indoor humidity makes occupants feel comfortable at higher summer temperatures
(2–3°F warmer setpoint possible). Dehumidifiers in humid climates can reduce "feels like" temperature,
allowing the thermostat to be raised. Each 1°F raise saves ~1% on cooling costs.

ECONOMIZER / NIGHT FLUSH
On mild-temperature nights (55–70°F outside), open windows and use whole-house fans to flush hot air
and pre-cool with free outdoor air. A whole-house fan uses 300–600W versus 3,000–5,000W for central AC.
Potential savings: 50–75% reduction in cooling energy on moderate nights.

DUCT LEAKAGE
Typical homes lose 20–30% of conditioned air through duct leaks. Sealing ducts with mastic sealant or
metal tape (not duct tape, which fails) can reduce HVAC energy by 15–20%. Focus on ducts in unconditioned
spaces (attic, crawl space, garage). Professional duct testing (blower door) costs $200–400; savings
recoup this in 1–2 years.

SOLAR + HVAC INTEGRATION
Use a solar-aware thermostat schedule:
- High solar production (10 AM–3 PM): Pre-cool aggressively while using free solar power.
- Low solar (after 3 PM): Let temperature drift up slightly to shed load.
- Peak pricing (5–9 PM): Rely on pre-cooled thermal mass; minimal HVAC operation.
- Battery storage available: Charge battery during solar peak; power HVAC from battery during peak hours.

HEAT PUMP WATER HEATER SYNERGY
Heat pump water heaters (HPWH) pull heat from indoor air — cooling it slightly. In summer, this provides
free supplemental air cooling while heating water. Run HPWH during solar peak hours for maximum solar
self-consumption and incidental cooling benefit.

MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE
- Monthly: Replace/check air filter (MERV 8–11 recommended)
- Quarterly: Check refrigerant sight glass; clear outdoor unit debris
- Annually: Professional tune-up ($80–150) — includes coil cleaning, refrigerant check, electrical inspection
  Returns 5–10% efficiency improvement
- Every 10–15 years: Evaluate system replacement (modern systems are 30–40% more efficient than pre-2010 units)
