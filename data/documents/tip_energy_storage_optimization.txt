TOPIC: Energy Storage Optimization Strategies
SOURCE: EcoHome Knowledge Base — Energy Storage Optimization

Home battery storage systems (like Tesla Powerwall, Enphase IQ Battery, or Sonnen) dramatically expand
your ability to shift energy use in time, reduce peak demand charges, and increase solar self-consumption.
Proper optimization of battery charging and discharging is key to maximizing their value.

BATTERY OPERATING MODES AND WHEN TO USE EACH

1. Self-Consumption Mode (Default Recommended)
   - Charge: Solar excess during the day
   - Discharge: Evening/night household loads
   - Best for: Net-metered customers with 1:1 or near-1:1 export rates
   - Expected savings: $400–700/year (10 kWh battery, 10 kW solar)

2. Time-of-Use (TOU) Optimization Mode
   - Charge: Grid during super off-peak ($0.07–0.11/kWh)
   - Discharge: During peak hours ($0.28–0.38/kWh)
   - Best for: Customers on TOU rates WITHOUT favorable net metering
   - Expected savings: $600–1,100/year (10 kWh, $0.23/kWh spread, daily cycling)
   - Note: Battery cycle life matters — aim for <1 full cycle/day to preserve warranty

3. Backup-First Mode
   - Maintains 30–100% charge reserved for grid outages
   - Best for: Areas with frequent outages or critical medical equipment
   - Trade-off: Reduced energy savings vs pure optimization mode
   - Recommended: 20–30% backup reserve for most households; 100% reserve only when storms forecast

4. Export Maximization Mode (VPP / Grid Services)
   - Battery discharges to grid during peak grid stress events
   - Earn $50–200/year from utility demand response programs
   - Platforms: Tesla Energy Plan, Sunrun Shift, Sunvault DR, Enphase IQ System Controller DR
   - Requires smart inverter with grid-export capability and utility agreement

BATTERY STATE OF CHARGE (SOC) MANAGEMENT
- Optimal daily operating range: 20–90% SOC for longevity
- Avoid repeated deep discharges (below 10%); degrades lithium cells faster
- Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) chemistry (Tesla Powerwall 3, some Enphase): Can safely cycle to 100%
- NMC chemistry (older systems): Avoid 100% SOC for extended periods
- Temperature sensitivity: Optimal operating range 32–104°F (0–40°C)
  Below 14°F (-10°C): Charging slows; protect battery in heated space if possible

BATTERY DISPATCH STRATEGY FOR DIFFERENT SCENARIOS

Sunny weekday with EV:
1. 6–9 AM: Grid charges battery at super off-peak (if TOU mode)
2. 10 AM–3 PM: Solar charges battery to 90%; excess powers EV charger
3. 3–5 PM: Solar waning; battery at 90%
4. 5–9 PM: Battery discharges to power home; no grid import
5. 9 PM–11 PM: Battery below 20%; small grid import at off-peak rate
6. 11 PM–6 AM: Grid charges EV at super off-peak; battery rests

Cloudy winter weekday:
1. 11 PM–6 AM: Grid charges battery to 80% at super off-peak
2. 6–9 AM: Battery discharges for morning loads (coffee, showers, heating)
3. 10 AM–3 PM: Low solar (5–15 kWh); battery charges slowly
4. 5–9 PM: Battery discharges for peak-hour loads
5. Result: Grid import only during cheapest hours; savings preserved

BATTERY SIZING GUIDANCE
- Standard household (no EV): 10–13 kWh covers evening loads + backup
- Household with EV: 13–27 kWh for both home + partial EV charging from battery
- Whole-home backup (1–2 days): 20–40 kWh depending on critical loads
- Stack multiple batteries for higher capacity (most systems allow 2–4 units)

Sizing calculation:
1. Track evening grid import (5 PM–11 PM) for a week
2. Battery = average evening import × 1.1 (buffer)
3. Example: Average 8 kWh evening import → 10 kWh battery is sufficient

DEGRADATION AND MAINTENANCE
- Modern lithium batteries degrade ~2–3%/year under normal use
- Warranty typically guarantees 70–80% capacity retention after 10 years
- Avoid: Thermal extremes, regular deep discharges, charging immediately after deep discharge
- Check: Annual firmware updates via manufacturer app; optimize charge curves
- Replace: After capacity drops below 70% (or at warranty end, whichever first)
- Recycling: Battery recycling programs available through manufacturers; do not landfill

FINANCIAL ANALYSIS TEMPLATE
For a 10 kWh battery system (cost: $10,000 after 30% ITC → $7,000 net):
- Self-consumption savings: $500/year
- TOU arbitrage savings: $400/year
- DR program earnings: $150/year
- Total annual benefit: $1,050/year
- Simple payback: 6.7 years
- 15-year NPV (at 5% discount rate): +$3,800

ROI improves significantly with:
- Higher TOU rate spread (>$0.20/kWh)
- More cycles per day (solar + grid charging)
- Rising electricity rates over time (+3–5%/year typical)
- Additional incentives (SGIP in California: up to $400/kWh rebate)
