TOPIC: Smart Home Automation for Energy Optimization
SOURCE: EcoHome Knowledge Base — Smart Home Automation

Smart home automation transforms passive energy devices into an intelligent, coordinated energy management
system. When devices communicate and act on real-time data (solar production, prices, weather), households
can reduce energy costs by 20–35% without sacrificing comfort.

SOLAR-TRIGGERED AUTOMATIONS
Create rules that automatically activate high-load devices when solar production exceeds consumption:
- "When solar export > 2 kW for 10 minutes → Start dishwasher"
- "When solar export > 3 kW → Enable EV charging at 16A"
- "When solar forecast is 80%+ for tomorrow → Schedule pool pump for 10 AM"
- "When battery is full AND solar production > 1 kW → Run washing machine"
These rules maximize self-consumption and reduce grid export/import.

PRICE-TRIGGERED AUTOMATIONS
Connect to your utility's real-time or day-ahead price API:
- "When price drops below $0.12/kWh → Schedule EV charging start"
- "When peak pricing begins (5 PM) → Set thermostat to 78°F and pause EV charging"
- "When super off-peak begins (11 PM) → Start water heater boost, resume EV charging"
- "When price > $0.30/kWh → Send alert, activate demand-response mode"

OCCUPANCY-BASED CONTROL
Use motion sensors, door sensors, and phone geofencing:
- Away mode triggers: Eco HVAC setpoint, phantom load shutdown, security system arm
- Return mode (15 min before arrival): Pre-condition home using off-peak energy
- Bedtime mode: Reduce lighting to 10%, set sleep thermostat, turn off entertainment devices
- Morning mode: Gradually increase lighting, pre-heat bathroom, start coffee maker

DEMAND RESPONSE PARTICIPATION
Many utilities offer demand response (DR) programs:
- Virtual Power Plant (VPP): Export battery energy to grid during grid stress events; earn $50–200/year
- AC cycling: Allow utility to cycle AC compressor 15 minutes/hour during peak events; earn $50–150/year
- EV smart charging: Pause charging during grid events; earn $100–200/year
Smart thermostats (Nest, Ecobee) and EV chargers (ChargePoint, Wallbox) have built-in utility program support.

WHOLE-HOME ENERGY MONITORING
Install a whole-home energy monitor (Sense, Emporia Vue, Curb) to:
- Identify devices in real-time via machine-learning device detection
- Track per-device energy consumption and cost
- Set energy budgets and alerts
- Find phantom loads (devices consuming >5W constantly)
Monitor installation cost: $200–500; typical savings from behavior change: $150–300/year.

INTEGRATION PLATFORMS
Recommended platforms for automating multi-device energy optimization:
- Home Assistant (open-source, local control, 3,000+ integrations): Best for privacy and customization
- Google Home + Nest Ecosystem: Best for HVAC + solar if using Nest products
- Apple HomeKit: Best for iOS households; strong privacy guarantees
- EcoHome App (this platform): AI-optimized scheduling with weather, price, and solar awareness

BATTERY STORAGE AUTOMATION
If you have a home battery (Tesla Powerwall, Enphase IQ Battery, Sonnen):
- "Self-consumption mode": Charge from solar; discharge during peak hours
- "Time-of-use mode": Charge from grid at super off-peak rates; discharge during peak
- "Backup reserve": Keep 20–30% charge reserved for power outages
- "Storm watch": Automatically charge to 100% before predicted grid outages
Optimal battery dispatch can save $500–1,200/year depending on TOU rate differential and battery size.

VOICE + AI INTEGRATION
Use voice assistants (Alexa, Google Assistant) or AI scheduling (EcoHome AI):
- "Hey Alexa, what's my solar production today?"
- "Google, set energy-saving mode for this week"
- EcoHome AI proactively notifies: "Solar peak in 30 minutes — good time to run pool pump and dishwasher"
- AI learns patterns: detects when EV arrives home and pre-schedules overnight charging automatically
